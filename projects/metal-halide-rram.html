Research / Metal-halide RRAM to neural network
Project

Metal-halide RRAM: from measured device to neural network

Taking lead-free metal-halide RRAM devices from our lab toward crossbar arrays and neural networks.

Scope
3network types: binary MLP, binary CNN and on-device learning
2benchmarks: MNIST and CIFAR-10
Tiledpassive crossbar simulation with wire resistance and ADCs
RRAMNeural networks

The question

A memory device that switches nicely in the lab can still fail inside a crossbar array. The question was how much accuracy a real, noisy, lead-free device keeps once thousands of them hold the weights of a neural network.

What I did

  • Fabricating the devices together with a teammate in our lab (ongoing).
  • Fitted conduction mechanisms and weight-update (LTP/LTD) curves to measured I–V and pulse data from our lab's devices.
  • Mapped every network weight onto a differential pair of measured device states, sampled from the real distributions.
  • Simulated tiled passive crossbars with wire resistance and limited-resolution ADCs, and checked the solver against an independent circuit tool.
  • Trained a binary MLP (MNIST), a 4-stage binary CNN (CIFAR-10) and a single layer that learns directly on the device.
